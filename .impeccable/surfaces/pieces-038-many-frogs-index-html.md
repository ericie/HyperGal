# Many Frogs

Mode: Experience. Autonomous, wordless browser artwork. User approved replacing
its childish casual-game sprites with a rough, physical, slightly unsettling
printed world, and requested fixing intermittent lag.

## Direction contract

THESIS: Small living bodies become pigment, and an indifferent moving landscape
continues to work on the marks they leave.

OWN-WORLD: Charcoal and mineral gray with an olive cast, ash machinery, muted
madder/oxide/umber/plum bodies. Narrow anatomical silhouettes and articulated
folded legs; strict overhead machinery. Worn contours, short incised lines,
fixed substrate pits. No smiles, white eye discs, toy side elevations or gloss.

FIRST VIEWPORT: The existing 21-column endless course fills the vertical view,
centered within quiet dark margins on wide displays. Terrain remains readable
as crossing bands, with softer boundaries and sparse worn markings. The frogs
are vulnerable moving marks; no controls, scores or new explanatory interface.

STORY / MOTION: Independent route decisions and hops, reproduction at flies,
traffic and river deaths, absorbing stains, tire transfers, current diffusion,
and the return camera remain. Hind limbs open during hops without a floating
shadow. Texture is attached to the world, never random flickering screen noise.

FORM: Code-led procedural Canvas 2D artwork, retaining one portable HTML file.
User-pinned direction wins over seed 83df2169; no new direction vote is needed.
No generated raster dependency or substitute static illustration.

PERFORMANCE: Cache deterministic terrain, directly solve periodic traffic
intervals, reject distant frog reservations before prediction, and rasterize
pigment impressions once (four additional diffusion stages for water). Stop
RAF while hidden. Bound retained stains to 640 and terrain print cache to 128.

VALIDATION: Desktop 1663×1000 and mobile 390×844 screenshots; collision-window
oracle, seeded baseline comparison, normal/reduced motion lifecycle, visibility,
resize and cache checks in scripts/check-many-frogs.mjs.
