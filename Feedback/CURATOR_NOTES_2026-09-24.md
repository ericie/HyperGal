# Curator's notes, early ideation for a show

**Date:** 24 September 2026
**Method:** every piece in `?status=all` (53 works) run headless at 1440×900 for one minute and photographed at 6s, 25s and 60s; thumbnails, READMEs and the August review read alongside. Pieces 034 and 047 needed a patient loader; 008, 009 and 045 needed CDP or canvas readback to capture.

I am not looking for a theme yet. I am looking for works that hold a wall, hold a minute, and are unmistakably this artist's.

## The room I would build

1. **Overgrowth (028).** The best single image in the archive. A white mount, a cut window, a wallpaper garden that climbs out over the institution that framed it. Bounded arc, no counters, no panel. The word windows (VOTE, HOPE) are on the nose; the shapes are better.
2. **Paddle (013).** The tightest idea here. One paddle standing where the net was, black and white, a rally that scores itself. Show it large and alone. Its slowness is the work.
3. **Only Invaders (005).** The one arcade riff with its own graphic identity: four coloured caps at the edges, ammunition rails instead of a score, and an ending. The boss round is an event, not a reset.
4. **Camouflage (027).** Full-bleed, hard-edged, the same machinery for the leopard and the army. Reads without a README from across the room. Best case in the set for a wall-sized projection.
5. **Growth Rings (015).** A piece that ends: 200 years in 44 seconds and then "cambium closed." The field-map aesthetic is now a genre; the ending is what saves it.
6. **Snake Pit (007).** The ground is the piece: a grey fossil layer of everything that has died, living neon on top. Ship it without the palette panel, which sits on the art by default.
7. **Soft Collapse (020).** In WIP, rated 25. At wall scale it is the most confident abstract image in the archive: Albers, Vasarely, textile. I would hang it tomorrow.
8. **Drips (039).** Commanding. It is also a Pat Steir waterfall painting, near enough that the wall text has to say so. Decide whether it is homage.
9. **Switch Stack (011).** Rated 5 by the artist. It is the only work where the viewer's body operates the piece: scrolling is the switch. Kruger and Wool energy. In a room it needs a wheel or a touchscreen, which is a feature.
10. **The three space fillers (050, 051, 052).** The most *drawn* things here. Silver on charcoal (Negative Fill) is the strongest. One wall of drawing machines, three screens.
11. **Mutual Command (009).** Poster authority from the two score bands alone. Loud arcade palette. Include if the show has an arcade room.
12. **Tideline (054).** Two days old, rated 10. Stratigraphic dye fields, two shores, no game, no panel. The most promising new direction in the archive.

**Rescues from the reject bin.** City of Signs (019) is the best draughtsmanship in the set; it is static, so print it. Forest of Rings (034) has real presence as a vertical slab and is killed by a heat-map palette. Cover Ups (037) has the best idea in the typographic group, the graffiti buff as painting, and an alphabet that cannot carry it.

## The rest, quickly

- **Clones with the player removed that do not transform:** Only Centipede, Vectrex Asteroids, Top of the Heap (Q*bert), Many Mario (bitmap anime clouds), Happiness Hunter (scoreboard and "TRUCK DISPATCHED" toasts). To Big for This World is a trademark silhouette in a room; a lawyer will see it before a viewer does.
- **Idents, not works:** Hypermedia Title 01 and 02.
- **Genre standards done well:** No Empty Places (Turing pattern), Mountains Waves and Valleys (the Unknown Pleasures plot), Patchwork Geometry, Knot Work (static, and centred with margins despite the README), Hyper-Lichen, Marbles (a stone showroom with a Rorschach), Growth Rings 2022, From Growth, A Record of Pursuit, Nothing but Flowers, Life Checkers (op-art punch, but Conway), Eyes Open (poster).
- **Invisible at room scale:** hunt prey paint, Many Frogs, Fuel Seekers, Moss Eaters. Cloudcutter, the artist's highest rating, is a superb idea with almost no presence in any given minute; one outline cloud and a dot.
- **Drowned by their own panels:** the Obstacles trilogy. The dry garden (032, rated 9) is one of the prettiest surfaces here. The river study (030) is a control panel with a picture behind it.
- **Elevator (031):** the five "rooms" are the same AI-looking server corridor and none of them is the flooded desk or midnight orchard the description promises. Keep it rejected.
- **Loaded Brush (053):** one photoreal stroke, no hand. As a subject, the gesture without a body, that is worth a second look. As a work it is a demo with a palette.
- **Persistent Positives (049):** ships with a "PP PERF TEST · v5.1" badge over the art, and reads as a greeting card.
- **Broken Patterns (041):** rated 80; in its first minute it draws three chords. The finished web is lovely. A room does not wait.
- **unbreakable, all-wall, The Greater Whole:** the first two are loading screens; the third is a wall text until someone touches it.

## What I want to see that is missing

1. **Bodies.** Fifty-three works and only two need a viewer. Everything else is watched. Nothing responds to presence, proximity, a second person, the room's light, the hour.
2. **Sound.** Not one piece. Paddle without a beep is a choice, but the archive has no position on sound at all.
3. **A duration policy.** A few works end; most loop. Nothing is long-form (a day, a week, a season). Nothing remembers a previous visit. An archive built to last twenty years forgets everything on reload.
4. **An author.** The gallery page is a title and four filters. No name, no statement, no dates, no account of what "HyperMedia Club" is. The strongest curatorial claim in the project, self-contained work that will still run in 2046, is nowhere on the wall.
5. **And that claim is not true yet.** Fifty of fifty-three pieces call an analytics server on load. Make the no-network promise real and say it loudly, or drop it.
6. **A wider source pool.** The arcade room is 1978 to 1982, American and Japanese cabinets only. The riff grammar (remove the player, invert the verb) is the artist's best tool; point it at something the artist did not grow up with, or at a game of the artist's own.
7. **The world.** Everything is abstract or arcade. The one photographic piece uses AI-looking images. No camera, no scan, no dataset, no place named. Growth Rings would gain from one real tree.
8. **Pieces that talk to each other.** Fifty-three separate windows. Snake Pit's dead could be Overgrowth's plate. A show of systems that share state would be a show nobody else can do with this material.
9. **Ugliness.** Everything is tasteful. The best pieces have a mean streak (Paddle, Mutual Command). More of that, less lichen.
10. **The reject bin as a room.** The gallery hides ratings and buries rejects. Curators love rejects. City of Signs and Forest of Rings prove the point.
11. **A work about the format.** Nothing here is about the browser, the file, the archive, or rot. A piece that only works offline, or that degrades on schedule, would make the whole project's thesis visible.

## Presentation notes for whatever is chosen

- Every default-visible panel, badge, footer readout and HUD comes off before the work goes on a wall (007, 010, 021, 022, 023, 030, 032, 043, 046, 049, 053).
- Scale test everything at three metres. Half the archive is desk-scale.
- Decide per piece whether reseeding is public. On a wall, "click to reseed" is an undo button for consequence.
