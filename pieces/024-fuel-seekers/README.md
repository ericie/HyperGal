# Fuel Seekers

A closed traffic system laid over a quiet resource map. The underlayer borrows
the domain-warped noise, folded currents, veins, halos, and mineral grain of
`Marbles`, but compresses them into a near-black oil field. Ten diamond fuel
pumps are selected from the richest points in that computed field rather than
scattered at random.

Forty-six spiral shells move continuously among the pumps. The amber level
inside each shell is its actual fuel: a full shell is filled to the top, then
its flat surface falls vertically as motion consumes fuel by distance. Nothing
is drawn behind them — the field keeps no record of where they have been.

A shell is a hermit-crab shell, not a body. It grows with use: every unit of
fuel it draws widens the shell a little and adds to its whorl, so an old shell
carries more, travels farther between pumps, and moves a little more heavily
than a new one. Freshly filled shells accelerate toward the most distant
pump-free pockets of the field, following long, gently swaying paths. At
one-quarter of their own capacity they take a direct line toward the nearest
open pump corner. Every diamond has four attachment points, one at each corner,
so as many as four shells can refuel there at once. The pump's own amber reserve
is a vertical level with a flat surface that falls as fuel transfers.

A shell that runs dry does not die. Its occupant is spent, the shell settles,
turns grey, and lies inert on the field with its aperture empty, waiting. Every
now and again a pump spends a little of its reserve to release an energy worm —
a bright swimming filament that crosses the field looking for an empty shell.
A worm that reaches one moves in, and the shell wakes with a small charge and a
grown body it did not have to earn. A worm that finds nothing wanders until its
charge disperses. The population never falls; it only shifts between inhabited
and inert.

Every transfer expands an erased patch along the local direction of the vein.
When a pump reaches zero, its diamond body bursts and the spent area is
permanently removed from the resource map. A replacement can grow only over a
different untouched deposit. Once the remaining field has no viable deposit, the
pump population thins instead of recycling old locations, and the field silts up
into empty shells that go on waiting for a worm that will not come.

Press `R` to generate a new oil field, relocate the pumps to its deposits, and
restart the population. Add `?seed=anything` to the URL to revisit a field.
With `prefers-reduced-motion` active, the system holds on a fully composed still
frame.

## Archival rule

This folder is fully self-contained. It uses browser-native HTML, CSS,
JavaScript, and Canvas 2D with no external assets or dependencies.
