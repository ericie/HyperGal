# Overgrowth

A directionless botanical wallpaper drawn entirely in Canvas 2D. Roses, peonies, poppies, tiger lilies, hibiscus, chrysanthemums, daisies, anemones, mophead hydrangeas, fringed Iris japonica, dwarf quince, honeysuckle, acanthus, green palm and fern foliage, and cream acanthus, rubber tree, Fatsia japonica, palm, and fern leaves are engraved onto aged rag paper in dye-pot colour — madder, weld, woad, walnut — with contour lines, curved hatch shading, turnover leaves, recurved prickles, and coiling tendrils. A faded silhouette underprint sits behind the growth, a ruled border frames the sheet, and a few specimens overgrow it.

The piece is laid down in three passes. First the plate: a dense wallpaper covering the whole sheet. Then the mount: a white board with a triangular window cut in it, dropped over the plate a beat later, bevelled at the cut and throwing a shadow down onto the artwork it hides. Then the overgrowth proper — every plant that follows is rooted inside that window, most of them against its rim and aimed outward, so the only direction left for the garden to spread is out over the board. The window is read as a polygon, so a different opening is only a different point list.

Nothing in the garden grows straight or observes an upright. Every stem, branch, and vine is built by the same curve walker, then turned into an all-angle repeat. The walker accumulates a guaranteed net turn plus a sinusoidal sway along its length; leaf blades bow on a curved rib, tendrils are logarithmic spirals, and even the border rule is drawn with a hand's wobble.

The first impression is deliberately dense and flower-rich, with extra blooms distributed across the repeat before the endless growth begins. Large off-white foliage prints late in the composition, varying the negative space between broad oval rubber leaves, seven-lobed Fatsia leaves, long feathered palm pinnae, and dense tapered fern fronds. Fresh specimens start roughly twice as often while keeping their measured individual growth duration: stems extend along their curves, branches wait for the main stem to reach their nodes, and leaves and flowers each pass through twenty drawn developmental poses. Leaf ribs lengthen before their blades broaden and fan away from the stem. Terminal buds flatten, sepals hinge back, petals lengthen and separate, and the flower centre appears last. Finished plants are stamped over everything already on the canvas forever. The pixels accumulate, but completed plant descriptions do not, so the endless growth stays memory-bounded.

Because every root stays in the window, reach is what carries the growth outward, and later specimens are given more of it so the mount is taken a little more of at a time rather than the colony stalling at one radius. Roots are placed around the rim on a golden-ratio walk, so every flank of the window is colonised at the same rate. The engraved caption and plate number are written on the board, the way a mounted print is titled below its window, and are buried in their turn.

Resizing or requesting a new arrangement starts a fresh plate, lays a fresh mount over it, and begins filling up again.
