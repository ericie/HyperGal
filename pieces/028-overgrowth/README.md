# Overgrowth

A directionless botanical wallpaper drawn entirely in Canvas 2D. Roses, peonies, poppies, tiger lilies, orange daylilies, hibiscus, chrysanthemums, daisies, anemones, mophead hydrangeas, fringed Iris japonica, dwarf quince, honeysuckle, acanthus, Monstera deliciosa, green palm and fern foliage, and cream rubber tree, Fatsia japonica, palm, and fern leaves are engraved onto aged rag paper in dye-pot colour — madder, weld, woad, walnut — with contour lines, curved hatch shading, turnover leaves, recurved prickles, and coiling tendrils. A faded silhouette underprint sits behind the growth, a ruled border frames the sheet, and a few specimens overgrow it.

Nothing in the garden grows straight or observes an upright. Every stem, branch, and vine is built by the same curve walker, then turned into an all-angle repeat. The walker accumulates a guaranteed net turn plus a sinusoidal sway along its length; leaf blades bow on a curved rib, tendrils are logarithmic spirals, and even the border rule is drawn with a hand's wobble.

The first impression is deliberately dense and flower-rich, with extra blooms distributed across the repeat before the endless growth begins. Large off-white foliage prints late in the composition, varying the negative space between broad oval rubber leaves, seven-lobed Fatsia leaves, long feathered palm pinnae, and dense tapered fern fronds. Fresh specimens start roughly twice as often while keeping their measured individual growth duration: stems extend along their curves, branches wait for the main stem to reach their nodes, and leaves and flowers each pass through twenty drawn developmental poses. Leaf ribs lengthen before their blades broaden and fan away from the stem. Terminal buds flatten, sepals hinge back, petals lengthen and separate, and the flower centre appears last. Finished plants are stamped over everything already on the canvas forever. The pixels accumulate, but completed plant descriptions do not, so the endless growth stays memory-bounded.
