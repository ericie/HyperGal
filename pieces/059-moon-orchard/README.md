# Lunar Founder

A self-playing pixel arcade patrol with cyan and blue mountains, green foothills, cities embedded in both parallax layers, peach ground, and one small buggy. Each replacement generation uses deep magenta, crimson, plum or rust body paint, shared by its fixed cannons, with all three tires in a darker shade of that hue and black windows and sprite cutouts. The body and fixed barrels have no outline; deeper warm paints contrast with the bright terrain. The buggy makes steady forward progress with gentle forward-and-back screen drift while the three landscape layers scroll at different speeds. It adjusts its speed and jump timing to avoid rocks, craters, and incoming bombs, checking takeoff and landing before committing. A safe ground-hazard hop keeps its planned throttle and launch time through landing; a new incoming threat can trigger a fresh plan. Original procedural sprites; no external assets or dependencies.

The buggy moves forward and back and has two fixed cannons: one points straight up and one straight forward. Both fire together from one trigger, with a shared 0.22-second interval. At most three paired bursts (six rounds) can be airborne. A burst keeps its slot until both of its rounds are gone; the cannons cannot rotate or fire independently. White rounds travel at 320 internal pixels/s. Small rocks break in one hit; large rocks take three. The UFOs use simple dome-and-disc sprites with different colors and proportions. They arrive in successive formations: yellow scouts, red bombers, fast cyan darts, then mixed groups led by magenta carriers. Each wave must clear before a short quiet gap and the next formation. Scouts and darts take one hit, bombers two, and carriers three; their speed, movement, payloads and attacks differ. Later formations get slightly faster, with up to six UFOs and four airborne UFO projectiles. Bombs leave craters. Growing foreground cities blast nearby turrets, rocks and holes.

Primitive deep-rust ground turrets stand among the rocks and craters, each built from a squat fixed pedestal and a simple pipe barrel. Both use one deep-rust paint with no outline, contrasting with the green hills and peach road. Their barrels turn toward the rover, elevate as it jumps, and fire red rounds with a short muzzle flash and recoil. A turret takes three buggy shots to destroy; nearby city growth destroys it outright. The rover can intercept its shots or jump over its base. New mounts only appear on clear ground, and bombs can destroy them by cratering their foundations. Turrets move with the road and stop firing throughout rebirth.

The middle and distant UFOs are quiet atmospheric scenery. They drift slowly and bob gently in their own parallax layer, without guns, bombs, hits or explosions. Distant UFOs measure 6–8 × 3 internal pixels; middle-distance UFOs measure 9–12 × 4. Their two-tone colors stay cyan/blue in the distance and green/shade in the middle. Background cities are fixed landscape artwork; they do not sprout or grow. Pause freezes all layers.

A fatal hit stops the road and all parallax scrolling. The buggy breaks into three wheels, chassis panels, cockpit, fixed barrels and axle pieces. Once every part rests and existing bombs finish falling, the parts grow into large alien towers, while one becomes a compact 44 × 34-pixel garage. Body fragments keep body paint and wheels keep the darker tire shade. The garage is narrower than the former 60-pixel facade. Its shutter rises over half a second, then the new buggy rolls out over 1.8 seconds. The buggy visibly clears the garage before road scrolling and combat resume. As foundations grow, hazards within or next to them erupt once: turrets and rocks disappear, and holes fill gradually into solid ground. The crater floor and sunken foundations rise together. Hazards beyond the growing city’s reach remain, and later bombs can open new holes. The buildings then scroll with the road, retaining their inherited colors.

The scene has no visible text, score, top bar, or saved state.
