# Lunar Landgrab

A self-playing pixel arcade patrol with cyan and blue mountains, green foothills, cities embedded in both parallax layers, peach ground, and one small buggy. Each replacement generation uses hot magenta, scarlet, electric violet or orange body paint, shared by its fixed cannons, with all three tires in a darker shade of that hue and black windows and sprite cutouts. The body and fixed barrels have no outline; vivid warm paints stand apart from the cool landscape. The buggy cruises around 19–28% of the screen width, with avoidance limited to the left 42%, and makes steady forward progress with gentle forward-and-back screen drift while the three landscape layers scroll at different speeds. It adjusts its speed and jump timing to avoid rocks, craters, and incoming bombs, using short-range reactions for the first buggy and predictive takeoff/landing checks after the first death. A safe ground-hazard hop keeps its planned throttle and launch time through landing; a new incoming threat can trigger a fresh plan. Original procedural sprites; no external assets or dependencies.

The buggy moves forward and back and has two fixed cannons: one points straight up and one straight forward. Both fire together from one trigger, with a shared 0.22-second interval. At most three paired bursts (six rounds) can be airborne. A burst keeps its slot until both of its rounds are gone; the cannons cannot rotate or fire independently. White rounds travel at 320 internal pixels/s. Small rocks break in one hit; large rocks take three. The UFOs use simple dome-and-disc sprites with different colors and proportions. They arrive in successive formations: yellow scouts, red bombers, fast cyan darts, then mixed groups led by magenta carriers. All attack waves enter from ahead, traveling right to left. Each wave clears before a varying 6–11-second quiet gap. Scouts and darts take one hit, bombers two, and carriers three; their speed, movement, payloads and attacks differ. Later formations get slightly faster, with up to six UFOs and three airborne bombs. All bombs fall straight down, with no horizontal aiming or glide; ships only drop while at least 65 pixels ahead of the buggy, with 2.8–4.4 seconds between drops. Bombs leave craters. Growing foreground cities blast nearby turrets, rocks and holes.

Primitive deep-rust ground turrets stand among the rocks and craters, each built from a squat fixed pedestal and a simple pipe barrel. Both use one deep-rust paint with no outline, contrasting with the green hills and peach road. Their barrels turn toward the rover, elevate as it jumps, and fire red rounds with a short muzzle flash and recoil. A turret takes three buggy shots to destroy; nearby city growth destroys it outright. The rover can intercept its shots or jump over its base. New mounts only appear on clear ground, and bombs can destroy them by cratering their foundations. Turrets arrive as singles or pairs: pair members are 1.8–3.4 seconds apart, followed by 16–32 seconds with no new turret. Their slower rounds travel at 105 pixels/s and shots are 3.2–4.8 seconds apart. Turrets move with the road and stop firing throughout rebirth.

The middle and distant UFOs are quiet atmospheric scenery. They drift slowly and bob gently in their own parallax layer, with occasional tiny vertical bomb drops and brief ground flickers. These use only their own layer colors and cannot hurt the buggy, damage ships, crater the foreground or grow cities. Distant UFOs measure 6–8 × 3 internal pixels; middle-distance UFOs measure 9–12 × 4. Their two-tone colors stay cyan/blue in the distance and green/shade in the middle. Background cities are fixed landscape artwork; they do not sprout or grow. Pause freezes all layers.

A fatal hit stops the road and all parallax scrolling. The buggy breaks into three wheels, chassis panels, cockpit, fixed barrels and axle pieces. Once every part rests and existing bombs finish falling, the parts grow into alien towers 10% smaller than the previous city, while one becomes a compact 44 × 34-pixel garage. Cities mix stacked pods, stepped terraces, twin spires, broad domes and narrow offset stacks; there are no white roof crossbars. Architecture changes between generations and background clusters, and the background cities are also 10% smaller. Body fragments keep body paint and wheels keep the darker tire shade. The garage is narrower than the former 60-pixel facade. Its shutter rises over half a second, then the new buggy rolls out over 1.8 seconds. The buggy visibly clears the garage before road scrolling and combat resume. As foundations grow, hazards within or next to them erupt once: turrets and rocks disappear, and holes fill gradually into solid ground. The crater floor and sunken foundations rise together. Hazards beyond the growing city’s reach remain, and later bombs can open new holes. The buildings then scroll with the road, retaining their inherited colors.

There is no scripted opening crater or scheduled death. The first buggy reacts to nearby ground hazards and can jump a simple ditch, but does not rehearse landings or predict bomb craters. After the first death, replacement buggies use the full avoidance planner; ground obstacles are spaced 4.8–8.2 seconds apart, and garage exits reset the hazard timers for a quieter return. The replacement leaves without an extra forward shove or forced hop.

The scene has no visible text, score, top bar, or saved state.

Click or Space pauses; Enter also works when the canvas is focused. S downloads a PNG. The canvas has an accessible description and pause state, with an inset keyboard focus outline. Reduced-motion preferences start the scene paused. Open `index.html` directly.
