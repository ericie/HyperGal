# Lunar Landgrab

A self-playing illustrated lunar patrol. Smooth procedural ink contours and flat fills replace the former pixel sprites: lavender sky and distant mountains/cities, turquoise middle hills/cities, a cream road and a small three-wheel rover. Flattened alien pods, substantial uneven stems, irregular windows, balconies, framed bays, classic saucers, round rotating tires and tumbling wreckage are drawn live with no external assets or dependencies. No visible text, score, top bar or saved state.

The distant terrain, buildings, atmospheric saucers and harmless decorative bombs stay in one lavender body/ink family. Back mountains use authored angular peaks and shoulders, one continuous silhouette fill and open skyline across matching 340-unit repeats, and sparse connected summit/shoulder folds. There is no sinusoidal ridge noise, detached slash detail or visible tile seam; mountain height, lavender paint, 0.18 parallax and cities are unchanged. The middle plane uses its own turquoise family. Foreground rover generations cycle orange, pink, violet and coral. Each tire channel is the rounded body sRGB channel multiplied by 0.55. Body wreckage and its buildings retain body paint; wheel wreckage and its buildings retain tire paint. Foreground contours use dark ink, and the two fixed rover guns use the exact body paint and flared roots that merge into the chassis. There are no gradients or glow.

One buggy cruises around 19–28% of logical width, with avoidance limited to the left 42%. The camera advances at 66 logical units/s; distant scenery uses 0.18× and middle scenery 0.46×. The first buggy reacts to nearby ground hazards and can jump a simple ditch, but does not rehearse landings or predict bomb craters. After its first death, replacements gain predictive avoidance. Safe ground-hop plans commit their throttle and launch time, with cancellation when an obstacle disappears or a new incoming projectile requires a new plan. The mature planner may rehearse deceleration from a committed jump boost when the entire remaining flight and landing stay safe. Hops launch at 157.5 units/s with gravity 294 units/s²: the peak is 30% lower (42.19 rather than 60.27 units), with the same 1.071-second level-ground airtime. Predictive padding remains 6 units normally and 7 during easing; only tall rocks (size > 18) cap it at 2. Physical collision padding stays 0. There is no scripted opening crash. Ground hazards follow five cyclic authored phrases: step-hop, rattle, double-hop, heavy and offbeat. Each phrase places 3–5 real hazards at fixed world offsets, then rests 198–264 world units after its last beat (3–4 driving seconds). The first starts after 0.6 seconds; the pattern clock pauses with the camera and preserves post-garage grace. `spawnGroundPattern(index = groundPattern++)` adds each phrase together as offscreen world objects.

Both fixed cannons fire together, straight up and forward, from openings aligned with the real projectile origins. One trigger has a shared 0.22-second interval and a maximum of three airborne paired bursts. A pair retains its slot until both rounds disappear. Rounds travel at 320 logical units/s, drawn as yellow ink-edged strokes. Cannons do not rotate or fire independently. Low chips (6–8 units) have broken low angular profiles and take one hit; medium rocks (11–14 units) take one hit and heavy rocks (24 units) take three. Authored pits are 24–32 units wide; bomb craters keep their existing behavior. Ground targets trigger paired fire only when ahead and within the fixed forward barrel height. Chips below the barrel require hops, preserving the paired-fire and three-burst rules without wasting shots.

UFOs arrive from ahead and travel right to left through five repeating formations: three scouts; bomber/scout/bomber; three darts and a scout; carrier/triad/dart/scout; then triad/bomber/triad. The four classic saucer styles remain; triads add three pale-turquoise spheres with orange cores and dark ink links. Scouts and darts take one hit, bombers two, carriers three and triads four; proportions, speed, hovering and payloads differ. Up to six UFOs and three foreground bombs can be active. Each wave clears before a varying 6–11-second gap. Bombs fall vertically and drop only while a ship is more than 65 units ahead. Ordinary ships use a shared 2.8–4.4-second drop gap. Triads bypass that cooldown, carry four bombs and drop at 1.1-second per-ship intervals, initially ready after `0.55 + slot × 0.24` seconds; they still share the three-bomb limit. Triad bombs start 14 units below the ship at downward speed 50, compared with the ordinary 8-unit offset and speed 32. All bombs keep zero horizontal velocity and gravity 48 units/s². Bomb impacts open craters. Pit voids have an open ink contour along their descending sides and bottom, with no line capping the mouth; collision and fill profiles are unchanged. Atmospheric distant and middle saucers hover and occasionally drop tiny harmless bombs within their own layer palette; they cannot damage the buggy or grow cities. Middle-layer saucers, bombs and impact effects render before the hills, which hide them behind the terrain. Background cities remain static artwork with open stem contours and unoutlined foundations, so distant and middle buildings have no bottom baseline; foreground foundation outlines are unchanged.

Rust ground turrets have curved pedestals and rotating cream pipe barrels. They track the buggy, elevate when it jumps, recoil and flash when firing. Each takes three hits; buggy shots can intercept turret rounds. New turrets appear only on clear ground, in singles or pairs 1.8–3.4 seconds apart followed by 16–32-second gaps. Their rounds travel at 105 units/s, with 3.2–4.8-second firing gaps. Bomb craters and nearby growing cities can destroy them. Turrets stop firing during rebirth.

A fatal hit freezes the road and all parallax scrolling. Three wheels and recognizable chassis, cockpit, barrel, axle and panel parts tumble, bounce and settle. When every part rests and existing bombs finish falling, the parts grow into source-colored alien structures. Non-garage towers and background cities use the established 90% scale. Close towers use kidney-shaped, tapered and mullioned apertures with unequal heights, offset placement, thin inherited-paint rims and sparse seams. Flattened pods and thicker trunks replace face-like rows, mouth curves, shiny streaks and knob antenna tips; background architecture keeps its simpler monochrome families. One fragment becomes a compact 44 × 34 garage with one offset mullioned window. Growing foundations blast nearby turrets and rocks, fill holes gradually and rise with restored ground; hazards beyond their reach remain. The shutter opens over 0.5 seconds, then the next rover rolls out over 1.8 seconds. It clears the garage before scrolling and combat resume, without an extra forward shove or forced hop. Hazard timers reset for a quieter return. Old buildings keep their inherited colors and move with the road. Atmospheric UFOs continue hovering during rebirth; pause freezes every layer.

Open `index.html` directly. Click or Space pauses; Enter also works when the canvas is focused. S exports `lunar-landgrab.png`. The canvas has an accessible description, pause state and inset dark-ink keyboard focus outline. Reduced motion starts paused.

Rendering uses `image-rendering: auto` and a display-sized backing bitmap with device-pixel ratio capped at 2. A context transform retains logical width `min(480, innerWidth)` and proportional logical height for physics. Export uses the backing resolution. Resizing reanchors active scene objects to their terrain layers.
